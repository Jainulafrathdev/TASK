Criteria To Be A Halal Stocks:

1.Nature of the business(To check what the company does)
  
   Alcohol: Companies that produce, sell, or promote alcohol.
   
   Gambling: Businesses related to casinos, betting, or lotteries.
   
   Pork products: Companies that sell or process pork.
   
   Interest-based financial services: Banks or financial institutions that operate primarily on interest (riba).
   
   Entertainment sectors: Adult entertainment or anything that is considered immoral or unethical.
   
   Weapons and defense: Some scholars argue that involvement in arms manufacturing for conflict purposes may be haram.
   
   
   => If the stock from above criteria is HARAM(forbidden) stock
  
  
2.Financial Practices (Avoid Interest and Excessive Debt)

   Interest (Riba):If company recieves  significant part of income as interest,then it is not halal
   
   Debt levels (Gearing ratio):If a company has too much debt compared to its assets or equity,may not 
                               be considered as halal Some scholars allows debt ratio <33%
							   
3.Shariah Screening Process

    Islamic organizations, like AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions) and 
  
    Dow Jones Islamic Market Index (DJIMI), have developed screening processes to determine which stocks are halal. 
    This screening process typically involves two steps:

    a. Sector Screening: To check the nature of the business
 

    b. Financial Screening: Once a company passes the sector screening,
                          it must also pass financial screening which is to check the involving of interest and debt levels.
                    
    c. Income from non-permissible activities: If a company earns a small part of its income from haram activities, 
     some scholars allow investing below certain threshold of 5%
     Investors are required to "purify" their earnings by donating the haram portion to charity.							   

4.Shariah-Compliant Stock Indexes

    Some organizations provide a list of stocks that meet Shariah standards. 
	These indexes make it easier for Muslims to invest in halal companies. 
	
	Examples include:

    Dow Jones Islamic Market Index (DJIMI)
    MSCI Islamic Indexes
    These indexes only include companies that pass both the business and financial screenings based on Shariah rules.





    Islamic organization that provides standards and guidelines for accounting and auditing of halal stocks and
    other Islamic financial products.    
		
		
	GLOBAL ORGANIZATION => AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions)
	
    * AAOIFI is the leading global organization responsible for setting standards on accounting, auditing, 
	governance, and Shariah compliance for Islamic financial institutions, including halal stocks.
	
	
	INDIAN ORGANIZATION =>Taqwaa Advisory and Shariah Investment Solutions (TASIS)
	
	* TASIS is one of India’s leading Shariah advisory firms, providing guidance on Shariah-compliant investments,
	including halal stocks.
	
    * They perform Shariah screening of stocks listed on Indian exchanges, ensuring compliance with 
    Islamic principles. TASIS evaluates the business activities and financial ratios of companies to determine
    if they are halal.
	
    TASIS also collaborates with BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange) to create Shariah-compliant indices like the 
	S&P BSE
	TASIS Shariah 50 
    which tracks halal stocks in India.
  
	
    
		
    The National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) has partnered with Shariah advisory firm Path Solutions to launch 
	Shariah-compliant indices. However, it is the Nifty Shariah indices that are most widely recognized. These indices 
	include the Nifty 50 Shariah Index and the Nifty 500 Shariah Index, which provide 
	a list of Shariah-compliant stocks for investors in the Indian market.
